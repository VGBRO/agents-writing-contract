# Stakeholder Update Policy

Route here from AGENTS.md when writing a status update, program update,
or cross-functional communication that reaches multiple audiences at once —
leaders, peers, and working teams in the same message or document.

## The core problem

The same update lands differently depending on who reads it. A leader needs
the decision signal. A peer needs the coordination point. A working team
needs the action. One message written for one audience fails the other two.

## How to handle multiple audiences in one message

Structure the message in layers. Each layer is complete on its own.

Layer 1 — the signal (2 sentences, for everyone):
  What is the status. What changes as a result.
  Every reader stops here if they need nothing else.

Layer 2 — the context (1 short paragraph, for peers and working teams):
  Why the status is what it is.
  What is resolved and what is still open.

Layer 3 — the actions (bullets, for working teams):
  Who does what by when.
  One owner per action. No shared ownership.

Leaders read layer 1 and stop. Peers read layers 1 and 2. Teams read all three.

## Rules

- Never bury the status in layer 2 or 3. If the project is at risk, say so in layer 1.
- Name one owner per open item. "The team will..." means no one will.
- Separate what is decided from what is still open. Do not blend them.
- Use the same status terms consistently: on track, at risk, blocked, complete.
  Do not vary them for tone. "Progressing well" and "on track" are not the same signal.
- Include a date on every open item. "Soon" is not a date.
- If the update requires no action from the reader, say so explicitly at the end.

## Status vocabulary

Use these terms and no others in a stakeholder update:

  Complete     — done, no further action
  On track     — proceeding as planned, no intervention needed
  At risk      — may miss the target without action; name the risk and the owner
  Blocked      — cannot proceed; name the blocker, the owner, and the date by which
                 the block must resolve
  Cancelled    — stopped intentionally; one sentence on why

Do not use: "in progress", "ongoing", "being worked on", "good progress".
These describe activity, not status.

## Length

Slack status post: under 150 words. Layers 1 and 2 only. Link to details.
Email update: under 300 words. All three layers.
Program review document: two-sentence opening, then one section per workstream.
  Each section: status term, one sentence on what changed, open items with owners and dates.

## Before/after

Weak:
  Quick update on the customer success program. The team has been making
  good progress across several workstreams. There are a few items we are
  still working through but overall things are moving in the right direction.
  More details to come as we get further along.

Strong:
  The CS onboarding program is at risk for Q3. Three accounts need
  accelerated onboarding to hit their value milestone before renewal.

  The blocker is implementation capacity — two of five slots are
  allocated. Resolution needed by August 10.

  Actions:
  - Varun: confirm capacity reallocation with delivery lead by Aug 8
  - Accounts team: send revised timeline to the three accounts by Aug 9
