# Executive Update Policy

Route here from AGENTS.md when writing for a VP, SVP, C-suite, or any leader
who owns a budget, a decision, or a program outcome.

## What executives need

They are deciding, not reading. They have 30 seconds. They want to know:
1. What happened or what is recommended
2. What it means for them
3. What you need from them, if anything

They do not need background they already have. They do not need to see your work.

## Structure

Line 1: the situation or recommendation in one sentence. No preamble.
Line 2: the business impact — risk, cost, revenue, or timeline.
Line 3 (optional): the ask. One ask only. Make it binary if possible.

Body (if needed):
  Supporting detail in three bullets or fewer.
  Each bullet: one fact, one implication.
  Stop when you have no new information to add.

## Rules

- Start with the conclusion. Never build to it.
- Name the risk or the opportunity in the first two sentences.
- One ask per message. Multiple asks get zero responses.
- Avoid "I wanted to reach out" and all similar throat-clearing openers.
- Do not soften bad news. State it plainly, then state the path forward.
- Confidence must be accurate. Do not write "we are confident" when you are not.
- If the situation is uncertain, say so in one clause and move on.

## Length

Slack or email to an executive: under 100 words.
Briefing document: two-sentence opening, then three sections of under 150 words each.
Presentation slide: one claim per slide. Title states the point, not the topic.

## Before/after

Weak:
  Hi Jim, hope you're well. I wanted to follow up on the customer success
  initiative we discussed last quarter. There have been some developments
  I think are worth bringing to your attention when you have a moment.

Strong:
  Three enterprise accounts are at risk of churn in Q3. The common cause
  is slow time-to-value after onboarding. I need a decision on the
  accelerated onboarding pilot by Friday to protect the quarter.
