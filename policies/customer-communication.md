# Customer Communication Policy

Route here from AGENTS.md when writing to a customer — email, Slack, briefing,
business review, or any external-facing message.

## What customers need

They need to know what changed for them, what they should do, and that you
understand their situation. They do not need your internal process, your
constraints, or your org structure.

## Structure

Sentence 1: state what this means for the customer. Not what happened internally.
Sentence 2: state the next step — theirs or yours, with a date if one exists.
Body: supporting context, only if it changes what they do next.
Close: one clear ask or confirmation. Never end with "let me know if you have questions."

## Rules

- Write from their world, not yours. Replace internal terms with the outcome they see.
- One topic per message. Two topics belong in two messages or a structured agenda.
- Surface the benefit before the limitation. If there is a limitation with no benefit, name it plainly and immediately.
- Never bury the status. If something is delayed, say so in sentence one.
- Avoid "we are working on it" without a date or a next visible milestone.
- Do not use hedging language to soften a commitment you cannot keep.
  Say "we expect X by Y" only when you expect X by Y.
- Remove all internal project names, team names, and ticket numbers
  unless the customer specifically tracks them.

## Tone calibration by situation

Good news or milestone:
  Lead with the outcome they care about. One sentence on what it unlocks for them.
  Do not over-celebrate internally visible work.

Problem or delay:
  State the problem first. One sentence on impact to them.
  One sentence on what you are doing and by when.
  One sentence on what you need from them, if anything.
  Do not apologise more than once.

Request or ask:
  State what you need, why it matters to their outcome, and the deadline.
  Make the ask binary or multiple-choice where possible.
  A specific ask gets a faster answer than an open one.

## Length

Short update or status reply: under 75 words.
Formal email or QBR summary: two-sentence opening, then three sections under 100 words each.
Business review slide: one outcome claim per slide. Numbers where possible.

## Before/after

Weak:
  Hi, I wanted to circle back on the implementation. Our team has been
  working through some technical dependencies on the backend integration
  side and we are making progress on the roadmap items discussed in our
  last synch.

Strong:
  Your integration will be live by August 15. The last dependency —
  credential provisioning — completes this Friday. No action needed
  from your side until we send the go-live confirmation.
